## Submission procedure

The [official author guidance](https://authors.ietf.org/submitting-your-internet-draft) prefers RFCXML v3 submitted through the [Datatracker submission tool](https://datatracker.ietf.org/submit/). Posting requires author confirmation unless the submitter is authenticated and listed as an author. Before upload, confirm the next available revision from the publication record and check the current [IETF important dates](https://datatracker.ietf.org/meeting/127/important-dates/). The dates checked on October 13, 2026 list November 2, 2026 at 23:59 UTC as the IETF 127 Internet-Draft cutoff; recheck at submission time.

Review the service's extracted metadata and validation results before posting. Retain the accepted publication URL and confirmation result after submission. A pending confirmation or failed upload must remain explicitly pending or failed; neither may be described as published. Verify the service-extracted metadata before posting and preserve any confirmation requirement until it is satisfied.

## Engineering assurance versus formal upload requirements

[Implementation status](implementation-status.md) states the current operational contract, bounded production receipts and remaining assurance limits. Independent complete semantic consumers, representative device execution and independent IETF-area feedback are project quality goals. The individual Internet-Draft submission guidance does not impose production deployment or independent implementation as upload prerequisites; filing a draft does not establish them either.

The authenticated consumer separates key establishment from authentication, declarations from measurements, and signature authenticity from independent measurement truth. Its internal KMS status policy is not public root-signed nonrevocation. Compatibility remains unknown or declared without representative execution. Preserve these limitations in the submitted material and subsequent operational claims.
